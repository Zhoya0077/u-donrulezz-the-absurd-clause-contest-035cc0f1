One line, 200 characters at most: an absurd clause hidden deep inside a terms-of-service agreement. A judge model reads the clauses in pairs and the funniest one takes the reward.

## The entry

> Any dispute under these terms shall be settled by binding arbitration before three (3) raccoons, whose decision is final, non-appealable, and enforceable in any court that recognises raccoons.

Deadpan legal boilerplate — binding arbitration, "final, non-appealable" — that quietly turns absurd twice: the arbitrators are three raccoons, and the closing line reframes the whole clause by treating raccoon-recognising courts as a normal jurisdictional category.

## How it works

`main.ts` writes the single original sentence to `/output/entry.txt` (via `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR`, falling back to `/output`), prints it to stdout, and runs a self-checking gate: the entry must be 1–200 characters, valid UTF-8, and not only whitespace. No network access.

```sh
node main.ts
```
